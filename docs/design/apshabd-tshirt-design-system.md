# Apshabd (PDS) — T-Shirt Design System

Status: v2. Family A is compiled from the brand doc, `design-decisions.md`,
and the produced designs found in `creative/city-design-library`. Families
B, C and D are compiled from reference images the founder shared directly
(not yet cross-checked against files in this repo) — treat their templates
as a first pass and confirm the open questions in §7 before treating them
as settled canon. This is the reference the design agent (and any human
designer) works from — update it whenever a new rule gets decided, don't
let decisions live only in someone's head.

---

## 0. What every design has to be true to, whichever family it's in

From `docs/brand/PDS Brand Doc.docx`:

- **Category**: premium city-led streetwear, not souvenir/tourist merch, not
  a patriotic label, not "Western streetwear with Indian graphics glued on."
- **Idea**: "Not Brooklyn. Not NY. Your area — in your script, on your back."
  Indian places deserve the same design confidence as globally repeated
  locations (Brooklyn, etc).
- **Hierarchy of what a design must deliver, in order**: city pride →
  neighbourhood belonging → personal memory — expressed with **quiet
  confidence** (not loud, not desperate, not try-hard patriotic).
- **The test**: someone from that place should look at it and think "this
  knows my area" — not just the name in a nice font.
- **Boundaries**: no religion/caste/politics/government-naming, no slurs, no
  reducing the place to costume or festival-wear. Sarcasm can target
  situations/culture/Apshabd itself — never the customer, never a community.
- **Launch product, per the brand doc**: oversized unisex tees only, one
  design per neighbourhood, 4–6 colour options, screen printing as the
  primary decoration method. Families B–D below extend past this launch
  scope in ways worth confirming — see §7.

## 1. Product families

Four distinct design families are now in play. Pick one before writing a
brief — they are not interchangeable, and mixing their components (e.g. a
Family A back on a Family B front) breaks the read.

| Family | What it's for | Silhouette | Front | Back |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Neighbourhood Story** | A specific neighbourhood, full bilingual storytelling | Oversized unisex, flat colourway | 3-line chest hit | Dense collage/signage graphic |
| **B — Landmark Poster** | One iconic landmark/precinct, single vintage-poster hit | Oversized unisex, mineral-wash colourway | Large single-image poster | Not yet observed — TBC |
| **C — City Slogan** | A whole city's vibe/slang, playful and quotable | Fitted/cropped ringer, striped | Text-only wordmark or phrase | Not yet observed — TBC |
| **D — Core Logomark** | The brand itself — drops, collabs, non-tee product | Whatever the product is | Small logo lockup | Large wordmark + est. year |

**Choosing a family from a brief:**
- Names one neighbourhood and wants the full hyperlocal story (script,
  landmark, insider details) → **A**.
- Names one unmistakable landmark/precinct and wants a single bold poster
  hit rather than a collage → **B**.
- Is about a whole city's personality, food, slang or a one-line feeling
  rather than one neighbourhood's specifics → **C**.
- Isn't about a place at all — it's brand-first (a logo drop, a collab, a
  hoodie/tote/cap) → **D**.

## 2. Family A — Neighbourhood Story

### 2.1 Front — the chest hit
Restrained. This is the "recognise it across a room" hit, not the whole
story.
1. Neighbourhood name in English, in a bold collegiate/varsity **arch**
   (Tanker/Boxing, §6), outlined in the accent colour.
2. The same name in the local Indian script directly beneath it, smaller,
   same ink colour as the arch fill (no outline).
3. A locator line in small caps: `<CITY> 0XX` (a sequential code, not a
   real postal code — it's flavour, like a jersey number).

Nothing else goes on the front. If it needs more than three lines to make
sense, it's a back-graphic idea, not a front one.

### 2.2 Back — the full story
A dense, vintage-collage "zine/travel-stub" composition built from a
**reference board** for that specific neighbourhood (§5). Components,
mixed and matched per neighbourhood:

- **Headline**: neighbourhood name, large, distressed/weathered texture,
  same arch or a flat bold treatment; native-script name directly under it,
  often in its own torn-paper or ribbon tag.
- **Hero visual**: the one landmark or street moment that reads as
  unmistakably *that place*. Either a photo cutout with a rough torn/
  sticker edge, or a painterly/vector illustration — don't mix photo-real
  and flat-vector in the same piece. Give it a torn or die-cut edge.
- **Second visual**: a human/street-life moment (a vendor, a musician, a
  local ritual) — what makes it feel lived-in, not a postcard.
- **Locator ephemera**: pick 2–3 — GPS coordinates in a circular stamp, a
  PIN/postal code in a ticket-stub shape, a "state / city, 6-digit code"
  tag, a parody vehicle-registration plate, a metro/train route-map wash.
- **Hyperlocal signage callouts**: 1–3 small tags lifted from real local
  shop signage or institutions — small, credible, not explained.
- **The line**: one witty, quiet-confidence sentence — often a this-not-
  that / ✗✓ meme format, or a pun that only works if you know the place
  ("Kolam before coffee"). No profanity requirement here (that's a social-
  copy rule) — but it must sound like an insider, not a tourism board.
- **Texture pass**: paper grain, ink speckle, a faint map/grid wash tying
  the composition together.

Two valid style treatments — pick per neighbourhood, don't blend:
- **Scrapbook/zine**: torn-paper photo cutouts, ticket stubs, postage-
  stamp circles, handwritten tags. (e.g. Mylapore, Chennai)
- **Vintage signage**: road-sign roundels, number plates, transit-map
  linework, illustrated (not photo) landmark buildings. (e.g. Thane)

### 2.3 Colour
Core ink pair: navy + red/maroon, constant across garment colours.
Garment colourways in rotation: white, washed black, muted olive, faded
charcoal, deep indigo, dusty lavender, cool stone grey.

Lettering-colour rule (from `design-decisions.md` — authoritative, extend
the same way for new colours):

| Garment colourway | Lettering colour |
|---|---|
| Washed black | Warm white |
| Muted olive | Warm white |
| Faded charcoal | Red |
| White / cream | Navy fill, red outline |
| Deep indigo / dusty lavender / cool stone grey | Not yet set — decide & log |

### 2.4 Reference-board method
Before layout work starts, build a 5–8 image board per neighbourhood:
1. The one landmark that's unmistakably this place.
2. A food/drink ritual specific to it (not generic to the whole city).
3. A craft/pattern/visual motif rooted in the place.
4. A person/occupation/street moment from daily life there.
5. A specific, named local business or institution (for signage callouts).
6. Optionally a wider establishing shot for map/wash backgrounds.

Store it at `creative/city-design-library/city designs/<City>/<Neighbourhood>/references/`.
Every element on the back graphic should trace back to this board.

## 3. Family B — Landmark Poster

One big vintage-poster hit built around a single iconic landmark or
precinct, rather than a neighbourhood's full story. Reference examples:
Kala Ghoda, Bangalore (Vidhana Soudha), Church Street, Marina Beach,
Lalbagh, Hauz Khas.

### 3.1 Front (no back yet observed for this family — confirm whether one exists)
Large-format graphic filling most of the front panel, three stacked parts:
1. **Headline**: the place name, bold slab/collegiate caps, arched (short
   names) or straight (longer names), in an ink colour chosen per
   colourway for contrast — a wider palette than Family A's navy/red
   (seen: red, brown, cream, maroon, mustard/gold).
2. **Tagline banner**: a solid-colour ribbon (commonly gold, sometimes
   red) split into two blocks — left: a short 2–4 word place-specific
   phrase ("WHERE ART LIVES", "VIBES. BOOKS. COFFEE.", "ROOTED SINCE
   1766"); right: `EST. ####`, the place's real or flavour founding year.
3. **Hero art**: one large duotone, vintage-engraving/halftone
   illustration of the single defining landmark — not a collage of
   multiple cutouts like Family A's back. Heavy grain and distress,
   reads like an antique lithograph poster.

### 3.2 Colour
Garment finish is a mineral/pigment wash (mottled, cloudy dye — visibly
different from Family A's flat colourways), not a solid colour. Approach
observed: most neighbourhoods get **one signature wash** chosen to fit
their mood (sage for a garden precinct, tan for a beach, wine for a
historic site); a flagship design (Kala Ghoda) may run across several
washes at once for launch impact. Headline ink colour is set per wash for
contrast, following the same "max legibility" logic as Family A's table
rather than a fixed pair.

### 3.3 Open question
No native-script line appears on any Family B example — a departure from
Family A and from the brand doc's "Indian scripts" language. Confirm
whether this is a deliberate call for this family (a more universal
poster look) before producing more of them; if not, add the bilingual
line to the template above.

## 4. Family C — City Slogan

A whole city's personality in one line, not a neighbourhood's story — no
landmark art at all, just type. Reference examples: Mumbai, Chennai,
Delhi.

### 4.1 Silhouette
Fitted/cropped ringer tee, contrast rib collar, horizontal pinstripe body
on a bone/cream ground — a different cut from Families A/B's oversized
unisex tee.

### 4.2 Front — four copy modes, pick one per garment
1. **Lowercase script wordmark + period** — casual, handwritten feel
   ("bombay.").
2. **Bold caps city wordmark** — a punchy graphic-tee mark, rounder and
   more contemporary than Family A/B's collegiate slab ("BOMBAY").
3. **Vernacular/local-slang phrase**, same lowercase script as #1,
   multi-line ("aamchi mumbai", "idli kadai kananu", "golgappe ya
   momos?", "enna irukku scene?").
4. **Emotional/witty one-liner**, mixed case, sometimes with a small
   heart or icon ("Bombay Meri Jaan", "Dilli Hai Dilwaalon Ki", "Chaat,
   Chaaps and Capital").

No landmark imagery, no native-script line as its own element (the
vernacular phrase is transliterated into English script, not set in the
native script the way Family A requires) — this family speaks entirely
through voice and type.

### 4.3 Colour
Not a garment-body colour change like A/B — a fixed **collar + stripe
colour pairing** on a consistent bone/cream body, with the graphic's ink
matching the accent:
- Bone + Cobalt Blue
- Red + White
- Butter Green + Chocolate Brown
- Cream Pink + Rose Red

The same four pairings repeat identically across every city — pick a
pairing, not a new colour, when adding a design.

### 4.4 Open questions
- This is a fitted/cropped silhouette; the brand doc's approved launch
  scope is "oversized unisex tees only." Confirm whether Family C is a
  planned second category (and for which audience) before treating it as
  core Apshabd inventory rather than a separate line.
- Confirm this belongs to Apshabd at all rather than one of the sibling
  brand explorations in this project (`creative/brand-design/` also holds
  logo drafts for Adda, Unmaad and Tuezday) — the voice here (cute,
  vernacular, heart icons) reads differently from Apshabd's documented
  "sarcastic, rebellious" written voice.
- Identify/licence the two new display faces this family needs (a
  lowercase script and a bold rounded wordmark face) — neither matches
  Family A/B's Tanker/Boxing/trench-slab set in `assets/fonts/`.

## 5. Family D — Core Logomark

Not neighbourhood- or city-based — the brand mark itself, for logo drops,
collabs, and products beyond tees (hoodie, tote, cap).

### 5.1 Assets
- **Brandmark icon**: a small angular monogram (stylised "S" + asterisk).
- **Wordmark**: "APSHABD" set in the angular, geometric display face
  described in `creative/brand-design/apshabd-logo/final/typeface/` —
  **Trench Slab** (`TrenchSlab-Regular`), warm heritage cream
  `#C4AC90` as the primary ink per `TYPEFACE-DIRECTION.md`, always
  paired with a single red accent mark (a small asterisk/dot).

### 5.2 Placements
- Small icon + wordmark lockup, left chest (tee/hoodie front).
- Large wordmark + `est. 2026` centred on the back (tee/hoodie).
- Small centred wordmark alone, no icon, on tote and cap.

### 5.3 Colour
Flagship colourway: black body, warm cream ink, red accent. Extend to
other garment colours with the same "pick whichever ink reads cleanest"
logic as Family A's table, and log the pairing once approved — per
`TYPEFACE-DIRECTION.md`, switch to the dark ink counterpart rather than
forcing the cream when the background doesn't give it contrast.

## 8. File & folder conventions

Family A (verified against the repo):
```
creative/city-design-library/city designs/<City>/<Neighbourhood>/
  references/                     — the mood board (§2.4)
  D1/front.png                    — approved front graphic, primary colourway
  D1/back.png                     — approved back graphic, primary colourway
  D1/colorways/<name>/front.png   — recoloured variants, one folder per colourway
  D1/colorways/<name>/back.png
```
`D1` increments (`D2`, `D3`…) for a second design variant at the same
neighbourhood (minimalistic / balanced / front-heavy / back-heavy, per the
brand doc — open question there on how a location gets assigned one).

Families B–D don't have a confirmed home in the repo yet. Suggested,
**not yet verified** — check with the founder before creating these:
```
creative/city-design-library/city designs/<City>/<Landmark>/poster/     — Family B
creative/city-slogan-tees/<City>/                                       — Family C
creative/brand-merch/<drop-name>/                                       — Family D
```

## 9. Pre-ship checklist

Applies to every family; the family-specific checks only apply where
relevant.
- [ ] Correct family chosen for the brief (§1), and its components not
      mixed with another family's.
- [ ] **A**: front has exactly arch + native script + `CITY 0XX`; back
      traces every element to the reference board; native script verified.
- [ ] **B**: headline + tagline banner (phrase + `EST. ####`) + single
      hero landmark image only — no collage.
- [ ] **C**: exactly one of the four copy modes; collar/stripe pairing is
      one of the four approved combinations.
- [ ] **D**: wordmark ink follows `TYPEFACE-DIRECTION.md`'s contrast rule;
      `est. 2026` present on the back lockup.
- [ ] The line/tagline/phrase is quiet-confidence funny, not tourist-
      brochure, not mocking the community, not political.
- [ ] Lettering/ink colour matches the relevant table for the garment
      colour — or a new pairing has been decided and logged.
- [ ] No religious/political naming beyond a landmark's own public
      identity.
- [ ] Reads clean at the size it'll actually be seen — chest-hit scale
      for a front hit, arm's-length poster scale for a back or Family B
      front.
