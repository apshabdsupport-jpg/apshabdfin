# APSHABD — City With Receipts

Status: recommended next carousel direction  
Format: 5 slides, 1080 × 1350  
Primary goal: comments that double as city-design research  
Secondary goal: establish the new 40-city design system

## Why this is the right next move

The project now contains 1,061 files and 882 visual/media files. The newest and most underused body of work is the 40-city front/back system created on 31 August. It is much stronger than another round of Dadar, Bandra, Colaba, and T. Nagar lifestyle photography.

The new system contains specific civic evidence: flower-auction times, taxi numbers, flood marks, night-shift schedules, ferry manifests, service counts, market ledgers, tile batches, public-works files, route diagrams, port signals, receipts, stamps, and bad handwriting.

That gives APSHABD a new argument:

> Generic streetwear prints a city name. APSHABD prints the evidence that the city actually happened.

This territory also avoids the language already repeated in recent work: `local`, `souvenir`, `pincode`, `city pride`, `skyline`, and `wear where you're from`.

## Winning concept

### A CITY WITHOUT RECEIPTS IS JUST A FUCKING FONT.

The visual world is not another cream/black/red lifestyle-photo punk collage. It is **municipal evidence maximalism**: public records, route boards, tickets, market ledgers, inspection stamps, handwritten bills, measurement grids, and garment artwork blown up until it becomes the environment.

Punk energy comes from scale, collision, photocopy degradation, torn edges, overprinting, staples, correction marks, and hostile copy—not from generic skulls, anarchy symbols, or western zine clichés.

## Caption

Most city merch starts with the name and panics immediately after.

A skyline. A monument. Some distressed type. Done. Fucking groundbreaking.

We went looking for receipts instead.

Madurai clocks the flower market at 04:30. Shillong keeps Taxi 001 moving. Indore starts a second shift when the shutters come down. Patna keeps the flood mark. Kochi keeps the manifest. Amritsar counts service. Kozhikode keeps the ledger. Mangaluru numbered the roof over your head.

The city already did the fucking art direction.

We kept the evidence.

Now give us yours: one token, timing, route, bill, rule, wall mark, handwritten sign, or dead shop everybody still uses for directions.

City + detail + proof.

Best fucking receipt becomes a back print.

APSHABD.

## Art-direction rules

- Do not use the Dadar flower seller, Bandra fruit stall, T. Nagar newspaper reader, Dadar skateboarder, or wet-night hanging tee in this carousel.
- Do not use a lifestyle model as the hero on any slide.
- Do not make cream/black/warning red the only palette. Preserve the city-system colors.
- Do not use a torn-paper collage as the entire idea. Paper is the subject; the content on it must stay legible.
- Use real asset typography and diagram fragments wherever possible instead of asking image generation to recreate dense text.
- Build the final slides deterministically in a layout tool so ticket numbers, city names, scripts, and CTA copy remain exact.
- Use the APSHABD wordmark once per slide, small. The archive should hit before the brand does.

## Other territories considered

### 2. TOURISTS GET LANDMARKS. LOCALS GET RECEIPTS.

Strong and immediately clear, but less ownable and less aggressive than the winning hook. Keep as a caption line or campaign sequel.

### 3. YOUR CITY HAS BETTER LORE THAN YOUR DESIGNER.

Funny, but it attacks designers more than it explains the product. Good standalone poster, weaker product system.

### 4. WE FOUND YOUR CITY IN THE FUCKING PAPERWORK.

Visually rich and usable, but more like a campaign title than a first-slide stop-scroll hook.

### 5. IF IT COULD HAPPEN ANYWHERE, DON'T FUCKING PRINT IT.

Excellent brand principle. Save it for a future manifesto carousel showing rejected generic designs beside accepted city-specific evidence.

`APSHABD MUNICIPAL ARCHIVE / FILE 001`

This can become a repeatable series. Each future file can focus on one evidence family: night shifts, water marks, market ledgers, taxi systems, public notices, port records, or names too long for forms.
